NOTE 1 - NATURE OF BUSINESS
Silverco Enterprises, LLC, Two Family Enterprises, Inc., and NCNT Holdings LLC (collectively
the “Companies”) operate a car and truck rental service out of several locations in the greater Los
Angeles, California region under the “Avon Rent-A-Car, Truck & Van” name.
Silverco Enterprises, LLC (“Silverco”) was initially incorporated in the state of California on April
23, 1984 and was converted to an LLC on May 17, 2022. Silverco serves as the primary operating
entity which rents vehicles to the general public.
Two Family Enterprises, Inc. (“Two Family”) was initially incorporated in the state of California on
April 14, 1994. Two Family owns revenue generating vehicles which it leases to Silverco.
NCNT Holdings LLC (“NCNT”) was formed as a limited liability company in Delaware effective
December 10, 2021. NCNT owns revenue generating vehicles which it leases to Silverco.
The Companies are wholly owned by MT Studio Services, LLC (“MT Studio”).
NOTE 2 - SUMMARY OF SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES
Basis of accounting
The accompanying combined financial statements are prepared using the accrual basis of accounting
in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America (“U.S.
GAAP”). References to the “ASC” hereafter refer to the Accounting Standards Codification
established by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (“FASB”) as the source of authoritative
U.S. GAAP. At times, the FASB updates the ASC with Accounting Standards Updates (“ASU”).
Principles of combination
The accompanying combined financial statements include the accounts of Silverco, NCNT, and Two
Family. The Companies are affiliated through a common owner (MT Studio) and shared
management. All significant intercompany balances and transactions have been eliminated in
combination.
Use of estimates
The preparation of combined financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires
management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the amounts reported in the combined
financial statements and accompanying notes. Actual results could differ from those estimates.
Significant estimates inherent in the preparation of the combined financial statements include useful
lives and the residual values for revenue generating vehicles.

Cash and cash equivalents
For purposes of the combined balance sheet and combined statement of cash flows, the Companies
consider all highly liquid investments which are readily convertible into known amounts of cash and
have a maturity of three months or less when acquired, to be cash equivalents. At December 31,
2025, the Companies did not hold any cash equivalents.
Restricted cash
Restricted cash is comprised of cash required to be held aside for California Department of Motor
Vehicles insurance purposes. As of December 31, 2025, restricted cash amounted to $76,800.
Accounts receivable
The Companies carry their accounts receivable at invoiced amounts less an allowance for credit
losses. The Companies do not accrue interest on accounts receivable. An allowance for credit
losses is maintained for estimated losses resulting from the inability of customers to make their
required payments. The Companies estimate the allowance for credit losses based upon historical
account write off trends, facts about the current trends, and other macroeconomic factors. The
Companies elected a practical expedient that allows the Companies to assume that current
conditions as of the combined balance sheet date will not change for the remaining life of the asset.
Additionally, the Companies have made the accounting policy election to consider collection
activity subsequent to the balance sheet date when making its assessment of the allowance for
credit losses. Subsequent collection activity was considered through April 9, 2026. Credit quality
is monitored through the timing of payments compared to payment terms and known facts
regarding the financial condition of customers. There were no changes to the methods or factors
that influence the current estimate for the allowance for credit losses during the year ended
December 31, 2025. Accounts receivable balances are written off against the allowance for credit
losses after all methods of collection are exhausted.

Prepaid expenses and other current assets
Prepaid expenses and other current assets consist of revenue generating vehicle registrations, prepaid
insurance, and prepaid property taxes.
Revenue generating vehicles and depreciation
Revenue generating vehicles are stated at cost, net of related discounts and incentives from
manufacturers, if any. Holding periods typically range from two to ten years. Generally, the
Companies estimate the period that the Companies will hold the assets, primarily based on
historical measures of the amount of rental-years-in-service or automobile mileage. The
Companies also estimate the residual value of the applicable revenue generating vehicles at the
expected time of disposal, taking into consideration factors such as make, model and options, age,
physical condition, mileage, sale location, time of the year and market conditions. Depreciation is
recorded on a straight-line basis over the estimated holding period. Depreciable lives are reviewed
annually based on management’s ongoing assessment of present and estimated future market
conditions, their effect on residual values at the expected time of disposal and the estimated holding
periods. Depreciation expense of revenue generating vehicles for the year ended December 31, 2025
amounted to $2,833,038. The cost of normal maintenance, repairs, and minor replacements are
recorded as an expense when incurred. Major overhauls or modifications are generally capitalized
as they occur prior to the revenue generating vehicles being placed in service. Gains and losses on
the sale of vehicles, including the costs associated with disposals, and depreciation of revenue
generating vehicles are included within direct operating expenses on the accompanying combined
statement of operations.
Property and equipment and depreciation and amortization
Property and equipment is recorded at cost, net of accumulated depreciation and amortization. The
cost of normal maintenance and repairs and minor replacements are charged to expense when
incurred. Major replacements or betterments of properties are capitalized.
Depreciation of property and equipment is provided using the straight-line method over the estimated
useful lives of the assets of five years. Leasehold improvements are amortized using the straight-line
method over the estimated useful lives of the assets or the lease term, whichever is less. Depreciation
and amortization expense of property and equipment for the year ended December 31, 2025 amounted
to $384,319.

Leases
The Companies determine if an arrangement is a lease at contract inception and reassesses that
conclusion if the agreement is modified. The Companies begin recording lease activity at the
commencement date, which is generally the date the Companies take possession of or controls the
physical use of the asset. Leases with an initial term of 12 months or less are not recorded in the
combined balance sheet and the Companies recognize lease expense for these leases on a straight-
line basis over the lease term.
Leases with a term greater than one year are accounted for either as an operating or finance lease
based on certain criteria. A lease liability and right-of-use asset are recognized at the
commencement of the lease. The lease term includes the non-cancelable period of the lease and
options to renew, extend, or terminate the lease that the Companies are reasonably certain to
exercise. The Companies determine the term of each lease at lease commencement and revisits
that term in subsequent periods if an event would require reassessment. The Companies have
elected to use the risk-free rate to determine the present value of future lease payments unless the
implicit rate is readily determinable. The right-of-use asset is initially measured at the lease
liability amount adjusted for lease payments made at or before lease commencement, lease
incentives, and any initial direct costs. Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities are recognized based
on the present value of lease payments over the lease term with lease expense recognized on a
straight-line basis.
Sublease income for operating leases is classified as a reduction of rent expense within operating
expenses and is recorded on a straight-line basis over the contracted term of the sublease. The
difference between sublease income recorded and cash received from the subtenant accrues as a
deferred rent receivable. During the year ended December 31, 2025, management reassessed the
probability of collection of the deferred rent receivable from the subtenants over the remaining
term of the sublease and has concluded that collection is probable.
Impairment of long-lived assets and goodwill
When facts and circumstances indicate that the carrying values of long-lived assets (including
operating lease right-of-use assets) may be impaired, the Companies perform an evaluation of
recoverability by comparing the carrying values of the assets to projected future cash flows in
addition to other quantitative and qualitative analyses. If the estimated future cash flows
(undiscounted and without interest charges) from the use of an asset are less than the carrying
value, a write-down would be recorded to reduce the related asset to its estimated fair value. No
write-downs were recorded for the year ended December 31, 2025.

Revenue recognition
The Companies’ primary source of revenue is from the operation of facilities providing vehicle
rentals to businesses and individuals. Other revenue includes the sale of loss damage waivers,
insurance products, fuel and mileage services and charges, and daily-rental charges for parking
space at various company facilities. The Companies recognize revenue from vehicle rental
operations when persuasive evidence of a contract exists, the performance obligations have been
satisfied, the transaction price is fixed and determinable, and collection is reasonably assured.
Performance obligations associated with vehicle rental transactions are satisfied over the rental
period and rental periods are short term in nature, with a maximum contract term of seven days.
Performance obligations associated with other rental related activities, such as charges to the
customer for fueling of vehicles and value-added services such as loss damage waivers and
insurance products are also satisfied over the rental period. Revenue from elements that are
charged to the customer, such as gasoline, vehicle licensing, tolls and citations are recorded on a
gross basis with a corresponding charge to direct operating expenses. Payments are due from
customers at the completion of the rental, except for customers with negotiated payments terms,
generally 30 to 60 days, which are invoiced and remain in accounts receivable until collected.
The Companies recognize receivables resulting from contracts with customers. Contract
receivables primarily consist of receivables from customers for vehicle rentals. The Companies do
not typically have contract assets or contract liabilities due to the nature of operations and invoicing
practices.
Taxes collected from customers remitted to governmental authorities
Sales taxes are collected from customers on applicable transactions and the entire amount is
remitted to the related taxing authorities. Taxes collected are recorded on a net basis and are
excluded from revenue and expenses.
In April 2024, NCNT entered into a repayment plan with the California Department of Tax and
Fee Administration related to unpaid 2023 sales taxes. The agreement requires monthly
installments of $21,774 over a 24-month period. In 2025, NCNT revised the repayment plan to
include the 2024 unpaid sales tax obligation within the consolidated repayment plan. The
consolidated repayment plan has similar terms and the monthly payment was increased to
approximately $41,000 per month. In 2026, NCNT revised the repayment plan to decrease the
monthly payment to $32,744. The 2023 and 2024 sales tax repayment plan balance as of December
31, 2025 amounted to $763,467, inclusive of interest and penalties. The current portion of sales
tax liability represents the amounts expected to be repaid in 2026 under the 2026 revised payment
plan.

Direct operating expenses
The Companies’ direct operating expenses include depreciation and gains and losses associated
with the Companies’ revenue generating vehicles, repairs and maintenance, vehicle logistics
(transportation, towing, impound fees, licenses, etc.), consumable vehicle parts and supplies,
salaries associated with employees directly assisting in vehicle rental activities and state use taxes
associated with the Companies’ fleet usage.
Advertising
Advertising costs are generally expensed as incurred. Advertising expense amounted to $1,270 for
the year ended December 31, 2025.
Income taxes
Silverco and NCNT are single member LLCs which are disregarded entities for U.S. federal
income tax purposes and profits and losses are reported directly by their sole member. Silverco
and NCNT plan to pay the necessary amount of distributions in order to satisfy the member’s
estimated income tax liabilities arising from their profits, if any. The state of California imposes
an annual fee on LLCs based on the level of gross revenue of the LLC and an $800 minimum tax.
The combined financial statements for the year ended December 31, 2025 include a provision for
the minimum tax and LLC fee within operating expenses.
Two Family is a domestic corporation that files a standalone U.S. federal and California state
income tax return. Two Family income taxes are accounted for using an asset and liability approach
that requires the recognition of deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax
consequences of temporary differences between Two Family’s financial statement and tax bases
of assets and liabilities at the applicable enacted tax rates. A valuation allowance is provided when
it is more likely than not that some portion or all of the deferred tax assets will not be realized.
The Companies recognize tax benefits from uncertain tax positions when it is more likely than not
that the position will be sustained upon examination, including resolutions of any related appeals
or litigations processes, based on the technical merits of the tax position. The Companies recognize
any accrued interest and penalties related to unrecognized tax benefits as a component of operating
expense. As of December 31, 2025, the Companies did not have a liability for unrecognized tax
benefits.
The Companies file income tax returns in the U.S. federal jurisdiction as well as in the state of
California, to the extent they are not disregarded. The Companies’ federal income tax returns are
subject to examination by the Internal Revenue Service for a period of three years from the filing
of a federal return. California income tax returns are subject to examination by the Franchise Tax
Board for a period of four years from the filing of a return.

Concentration of credit risk
Financial instruments which potentially subject the Companies to concentration of credit risk
consist primarily of cash, restricted cash and accounts receivable. The Companies maintain their

excess of federally insured limits. At December 31, 2025, no customer comprised greater than ten

generally mitigated by the Companies’ credit policies and the overall credit worthiness of its
customers.
Geographic concentration
The Companies have a significant geographic concentration in the state of California. Substantially
all revenues for the year ended December 31, 2025 comprised of customers in the state of
California.
Economic and operational risk for film and entertainment industry customers
The Companies depend on revenue generated from usage of revenue generating vehicles by the film
and entertainment industry. For the year ended December 31, 2025, approximately 39% of revenue
was derived from customers operating within this industry.
Variable interest entities

Interest Entities, the Companies made the accounting policy election to not apply the variable interest
entity guidance to legally related entities if both the parent and the legal entity being evaluated for
consolidating meet specific criteria.

NOTE 6 - NOTES PAYABLE
In May 2022, Two Family assumed outstanding lending arrangements with 1st Source Bank to permit
borrowing, subject to bank approval, to acquire revenue generating vehicles. In May 2022, NCNT
entered into a similar arrangement with 1st Source Bank. No further advances were made to Two
Family and instead all notes and borrowings were issued to NCNT. The notes issued to NCNT could
not exceed $29 million. The principal amount, interest rate, payment amount, payment due dates, the
maturity date and other particulars for each loan were set forth in a promissory note, addendum,
schedule or other separate document containing such terms (each a “Note”). NCNT and Two Family
were provided with the option of choosing either a fixed interest rate or a floating interest rate at the
loan funding date for each such Note. Floating interest rates were charged at a rate equal to the prime
rate, as published in the Wall Street Journal, plus an interest rate margin of 2.75% with a floor of 7.50%
per annum. Fixed interest rates were determined by the lender at the date of loan funding. The monthly
amortization of the Notes were dependent on the make and model of the revenue generating vehicle
purchased and ranged from 2.00% to 3.00% per month.
In December 2024, the Companies entered into a reschedule agreement which amended the schedule
of payments under the lending arrangements (for both Two Family and NCNT), such that beginning
November 25, 2024, interest-only payments were due November and December, annually. Monthly
payments of principal equal to 2.00% of the original amount advanced on the remaining collateral plus
interest on the entire principal balance at the then-applicable interest rate, were due January through
October annually, commencing on January 25, 2025 and then on the same day of each succeeding
month through the maturity dates. The Notes matured at various dates through October 2029.
In December 2025, Two Family and NCNT consolidated outstanding lending agreements with 1st
Source Bank in the amount of $5,044,955 and $20,730,114, respectively (“Notes”). Monthly payments
of principal and interest begin February 2026 and the entire unpaid principal balance, including
interest, shall be due in August 2027. Fixed interest rates are charged at 9.25% per annum.
In December 2025, the Companies entered into a promissory note with 1st Source Bank (“Notes”) in
the amount of $2,000,000. The unpaid principal balance and interest, if not sooner paid or declared to
be due in accordance with the terms, shall be due and payable in full in August 2027. No interest rate
is charged.
The Notes are secured by all assets advanced against and are cross collateralized by and amongst the
Companies. The Notes are also guaranteed by Silverco and related party entities. The unpaid principal
balance for the 1st Source Bank Notes as of December 31, 2025 amounted to $27,712,981.
In January 2024, NCNT entered into a note payable agreement with US Bank in order to secure
additional financing to acquire revenue generating vehicles for operations, separate from the 1st Source
Bank agreement. The note amounted to $162,219, has a five-year term, bears a fixed interest rate of
7.85% per annum, and requires monthly payment amounting to $3,278. The note payable is secured
by all assets advanced against. The unpaid principal balance for the US Bank loan as of December 31,
2025 amounted to $107,401 and is included as part of the notes payable balances within the
accompanying combined balance sheet.

secured by substantially all of the Companies’ assets and provide revolving advances with a combined

long term on the accompanying combined balance sheet. All other line of credit balances are due on
demand, as the original maturities dates have passed, and are presented as current liabilities on the
accompanying combined balance sheet. All lines of credit are subordinated to the 1st Source Bank
notes. As of December 31, 2025, the outstanding lines of credit balance amounted to $4,904,772,

$738,787. As of December 31, 2025, accrued interest related to the lines of credit amounted to

NOTE 13 - GOING CONCERN
The Companies’ management has identified conditions or events that give rise to substantial doubt
about the Companies’ ability to continue as a going concern. On a combined basis, the Companies
incurred substantial losses from operations during the year, are in a negative working capital
position and are reporting a significant owner’s deficit as of December 31, 2025. These conditions
raise substantial doubt about the Companies’ ability to continue as a going concern.
Management has implemented a plan to strengthen its cash flow, liquidity and financial position,
including cost cutting initiatives and negotiating favorable terms with the Companies’ third party
and related party lenders, as well as pursuing complementary acquisitions of businesses within
their industry to expand the Companies’ revenue base and customer relationships. Management
believes the plan to improve operating performance and strengthening future cash flows will
position the Companies to be able to meet their obligations as they come due in the ordinary course
of business for the 12-month period following the date the combined financial statements were
available to be issued. However, there can be no assurance that the Companies’ plans to improve
their operating performance and financial position will be successful. Any inability to execute the
mitigating plans could have an adverse effect on the business and their operations and the effect
could be material. Due to the uncertainty surrounding the ability of the Companies to successfully
execute the mitigating plans, there is substantial doubt that the Companies will be able to continue
as a going concern. The accompanying combined financial statements do not include any
adjustments that might be necessary if the Companies are unable to continue as a going concern.
